# Attribution and source boundaries

## Original work in this repository

The skill instructions, source analyses, teaching examples, validator, and SVG banner are independently written or drawn for this project. They are covered by the repository's [MIT License](LICENSE).

The examples are hypothetical teaching inputs. Their numbers and technical setups are not results from the source papers. No paper PDF, paper figure, long excerpt, user manuscript, or experimental dataset is included.

## Research-paper corpus

The [manifest](skills/revise-research-writing/references/corpus-manifest.json) identifies 30 collaborative papers and the particular public versions used for writing observations. The [source guide](skills/revise-research-writing/references/sources-and-patterns.md) gives short mechanism descriptions and locations. H01–H15 select papers with Kaiming He; X01–X15 select papers with Saining Xie. Author position is a selection criterion, not evidence of who composed any sentence.

Paper copyrights remain with their respective rights holders. Linking a paper or discussing a general writing technique does not relicense the paper. Neither the authors, their institutions, publishers, nor conference organizers endorse this project.

## Editorial and skill-design influences

[Reference integration](skills/revise-research-writing/references/reference-integration.md) identifies the consulted projects, exact files where available, and what was adopted or deliberately not adopted. These include writing-dna-skill; Anthropic and OpenAI skill-creator; humanizer; K-Dense scientific-writing; Orchestra's writing guide; and selected community Nature-related tools.

The official [Nature summary-paragraph guide](https://www.nature.com/documents/nature-summary-paragraph.pdf) informs a limited discussion of information function. It does not make this an official Nature skill. Community projects with “Nature” in their names are not treated as official Nature sources.

No code, original examples, or substantial passages from those repositories are redistributed. Their licenses are recorded as source context, not used to claim rights over their third-party paper quotations. Any future contribution that copies or substantively adapts licensed material must preserve the applicable notices and document its provenance.

## Why MIT

This package is intended to be copied, adapted, and embedded in writing workflows. A single permissive license keeps that use straightforward and requires retention of its notice. The choice applies only to original repository content; external works retain their own terms. It provides no promise about publication acceptance, model behavior, or writing quality.
